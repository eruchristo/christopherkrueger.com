 Communication and Support Guidance

Purpose

This document provides essential information to help authorities communicate effectively and safely with an individual diagnosed with Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorder (FASD). Difficulties described below are neurological in origin and are not willful, oppositional, or deceptive.



Key Cognitive and Communication Challenges

Memory Impairments
• Significant short-term and long-term memory difficulties
• May forget instructions immediately after hearing them
• May appear inconsistent because past learning does not reliably carry over
Communication Disorder
• Difficulty processing complex or rapid speech
• Trouble expressing thoughts clearly, especially under stress
• May agree or say “yes” without fully understanding
Abstract Thinking Difficulties
• Limited understanding of abstract concepts such as rules, time, intent, rights, or hypotheticals
Cause-and-Effect Difficulties
• Struggles to connect actions with consequences, especially delayed consequences
• Poor understanding of “if–then” reasoning
• Consequences do not reliably change future behavior
Learning Disabilities
 • Elementary school reading level



Effective Communication Guidelines

Use Clear, Concrete Language
• Speak slowly and calmly
• Use short, literal sentences
• Avoid idioms, metaphors, sarcasm, or implied meaning
Give One Instruction at a Time
• Break tasks into single steps
• Do not stack instructions
• Pause between steps
Check for Understanding
• Ask him to repeat instructions in his own words
• Do not rely on “Do you understand?”
• Re-explain as needed without penalty or frustration
Provide External Structure
• Use visual cues or physical demonstrations
• Repeat information consistently and patiently
Allow Extra Processing Time
• Silence is processing, not necessarily resistance
• Do not rush answers or decisions
• Avoid rapid questioning
Minimize Stress
• Calm environments improve comprehension
• Raised voices, threats, or pressure degrade understanding
• Compliance under stress does not equal comprehension


What Does Not Work
• Assuming prior warnings or consequences were understood or remembered
• Expecting learning through punishment
• Interpreting confusion as dishonesty
• Using abstract explanations or moral reasoning
